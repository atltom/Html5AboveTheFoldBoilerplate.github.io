Engineered Retaining Walls for Grade Stabilization & Elevation Changes

Retaining walls are vital civil structures that transform steep, unusable slopes into functional flat lawns, terraced gardens, and secure patio foundations. At S. Phaneuf & Associates, we build structural and decorative retaining walls throughout Massachusetts engineered to hold thousands of tons of earth while enhancing your property's landscape architecture.

Hydrostatic pressure from trapped groundwater is the primary cause of retaining wall failure across New England. Our civil construction crews engineer every wall with a clean 3/4-inch angular crushed stone backfill drainage column, continuous perforated French drain piping, and heavy geotextile separation fabric. For walls exceeding four feet or supporting driveways and patios, we mechanically tie the wall into the hillside using high-tensile biaxial geogrid reinforcement.

Whether your site requires heavy commercial segmental concrete blocks, traditional hand-chiseled New England fieldstone, or dramatic glacier boulder walls, S. Phaneuf & Associates has the heavy machinery and artisan stonework expertise to build retaining structures that last for generations.

Engineered Segmental Concrete Block Retaining Walls

Segmental retaining wall (SRW) systems—such as Allan Block, Unilock, and Techo-Bloc—are ideal for structural slope stabilization, parking lot retainage, and multi-tier patio support. Interlocking fiberglass pins and rear lips provide exceptional shear resistance, allowing us to build walls over 15 feet tall when paired with geogrid reinforcement.

Segmental Block Highlights

  • Engineered for maximum structural load bearing and tall slope retention
  • Available in split-face, weathered stone, and contemporary smooth finishes
  • Built-in setbacks provide consistent, engineered inward batter
  • Backed by commercial structural engineering calculations and manufacturer warranties

Authentic New England Dry-Laid Fieldstone Walls

Nothing captures the historic charm of Massachusetts like an authentic fieldstone wall. Our master stonemasons hand-chisel weathered New England granite and round fieldstone, selecting each stone for balance, friction, and tight horizontal joinery. Dry-laid walls naturally drain moisture and flex during seasonal freeze-thaw cycles without fracturing.

Structural Engineering & Drainage Standards

A retaining wall is a civil retaining structure. S. Phaneuf & Associates adheres to strict engineering protocols to prevent wall tipping, sliding, and blowout.

Compacted Aggregate Leveling Trench

We excavate a trench 18 to 24 inches wide, remove native subsoils, and compact a minimum 6-inch base of dense-graded crushed gravel. The first course of stone or block is buried below finished grade to prevent toe kick-out.

Clean 3/4-Inch Crushed Stone Drainage Column

We place a minimum 12-inch wide vertical zone of washed 3/4-inch crushed stone directly behind the wall face. This aggregate zone allows water to drop immediately to the foundation pipe rather than building hydraulic pressure against the wall face.

Perforated French Drain & Geotextile Envelope

A continuous 4-inch perforated PVC or corrugated drainage pipe is installed at the heel of the wall footing. The stone drainage core is wrapped in non-woven geotextile filter fabric, preventing fine silts from clogging the drainage system.

Biaxial Geogrid Soil Reinforcement

For walls over 4 feet tall or walls supporting surcharges, we install high-tensile polyester geogrid sheets between block courses, extending 4 to 8 feet back into the retained hillside. The compacted fill locks into the geogrid, turning the earth mass into a unified reinforced structure.

Our Structural Retaining Wall Construction Process

Our systematic installation workflow guarantees structural safety and lasting stability.

1. Elevation Survey & Soil Analysis

We survey the site using optical levels, analyze soil conditions (clay, sand, ledge), determine wall heights, and calculate surcharge loads.

2. Heavy Excavation & Foundation Trenching

Our excavators dig down to stable subgrade, benching the hillside slope and preparing the leveling pad trench below grade.

3. Crushed Gravel Pad & Base Course Placement

We install and compact the crushed aggregate foundation pad and lay the bottom course of stone or blocks with absolute laser level accuracy.

4. Drainage Core, Geogrid & Lift Backfilling

As courses rise, we install the perforated drain pipe, backfill the 3/4" stone drainage zone, place geogrid reinforcement, and compact soil behind the wall in 6-inch lifts.

5. Wall Coping & Final Grade Integration

We secure heavy cap units using high-strength masonry adhesive, grade topsoil behind the wall for positive surface runoff, and restore surrounding terrain.

Frequently Asked Questions About Retaining Walls

When does a retaining wall require an engineered building permit in Massachusetts?

Under the Massachusetts State Building Code (780 CMR), any retaining wall that retains more than 4 feet of unbalanced fill—or any wall supporting a surcharge load such as a driveway, patio, or building—requires engineered design plans stamped by a registered professional engineer (PE) and a municipal building permit. S. Phaneuf & Associates coordinates all structural engineering, soil testing, and local permitting.

Why do poorly built retaining walls fail in New England?

The number one cause of retaining wall failure is hydrostatic pressure. When groundwater builds up behind an unvented wall during winter freezes, the expanding ice exerts thousands of pounds of lateral force, causing the wall to bow, tilt, or collapse. We prevent this by installing a 12-inch drainage column of clean 3/4-inch crushed stone directly behind the wall face, wrapped in geotextile fabric with a perforated drain pipe daylighted to gravity.

What is the difference between dry-laid and mortared stone walls?

Dry-laid fieldstone walls rely on gravity, stone interlocking, and backward batter (inward slope). Because they have no rigid mortar, they can flex slightly with seasonal ground freeze-thaw without cracking. Mortared walls provide a formal, continuous masonry look but require a poured concrete footing below the frost line (48 inches in Massachusetts) to prevent structural cracking.

Can you combine retaining walls with stone stairs and integrated landscape lighting?

Yes. Most of our residential wall installations feature inset solid granite steps or bluestone tread staircases and low-voltage hardscape LED under-cap lighting. We run internal conduits behind the wall core during assembly for a clean, wire-free finish.

How long does an engineered retaining wall last?

When built with proper aggregate drainage and geogrid soil stabilization, a structural segmental block or boulder retaining wall has an expected service life of 50 to 100+ years.
